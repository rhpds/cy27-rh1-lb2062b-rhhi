= Conclusion and Next Steps

[[the-chain]]
== The Trust Chain in Summary

Every artifact you worked with in this lab is connected:

. A low-noise base image (zero CVEs at ship time) -- chosen before the application is built.
. An SBOM -- a machine-readable inventory of every package in the image.
. A signature -- proof the image hasn't changed since it was signed.
. A signed SBOM attestation -- proof the SBOM belongs to this specific image digest.
. Build provenance -- a record of the builder identity, environment controls, and every pinned input.

[[what-you-learned]]
== What You've Learned

* *Module 1 — Vulnerability Scanning*: A clean base image eliminates scanner noise before you write a line of application code. Zero CVEs means every finding that appears after is yours to own.

* *Module 2 — Working with SBOMs*: An SBOM turns an opaque image into an auditable inventory. Without it, a signature only proves the image hasn't changed -- it can't say what's inside.

* *Module 3 — Verifying Published Signatures*: Trusting images requires the ability to verify what exists on disk is the same thing as in the registry. Using cryptographically signatures makes this simple.

* *Module 4 — Image Mirroring Setup*: Internal tooling doesn't need to break the chain of trust, a little setup and process can include your delivery infrastructre in the provenance of an image.

* *Module 5 — Image Signing and Verification*: Creating signatures for images is important for your own applications, but also adding custody indications to imported images. 

* *Module 6 — Image SBOM Attestation*: Attestation upgrades a signature from identity proof to evidence carrier. The SBOM is no longer a separate file -- it's cryptographically bound to this exact image digest.

* *Module 7 — Establishing Supply Chain Trust*: Provenance closes the final gap: you can trace an image back to a specific source commit, builder identity, and hermetic build environment. That's the claim a vendor makes for every signed image -- and the standard you can now require from any of them.

[[next-steps]]
== Next Steps

The techniques in this lab apply wherever you build and ship containers.

*Integrate into your CI/CD pipeline*

* Run scanners on every build. Fail the pipeline on critical CVEs above your threshold.
* Add signatures and attestation as post-build steps.
* Extend those policies to require a verified SBOM attestation for running compliance-sensitive workloads.

*Explore the Red Hat ecosystem*

* https://access.redhat.com/products/red-hat-quay[Red Hat Quay^] stores Cosign signatures and SBOM attachments natively alongside images.
* https://access.redhat.com/products/red-hat-advanced-cluster-security-for-kubernetes[Red Hat Advanced Cluster Security (RHACS)^] integrates vulnerability scanning and image signing policy enforcement.
* https://access.redhat.com/documentation/en-us/red_hat_openshift_pipelines[Red Hat OpenShift Pipelines^] with Tekton Chains generates SLSA provenance for your builds automatically -- the same provenance you inspected in Module 7.
